# Benchmarking the docs against competitors

Use this before writing any page whose job is to **win a customer**, not just instruct one: the introduction, the quick start, feature overviews, migration pages.

The goal is never to copy their copy. It is to find (a) the structural patterns that reduce drop-off, and (b) the things we can say truthfully that they can't.

---

## Who to check

| Competitor | Where to start | Why it's on the list |
|---|---|---|
| **ManyChat** | `help.manychat.com` | The category leader for Instagram comment-to-DM automation. The pattern most readers already know. |
| **LinkDM** | `linkdm.com` | Instagram-only, creator-focused, "send a link when someone comments" positioning. |
| **CreatorFlow** | `creatorflow.so` | Creator-focused Instagram DM automation. |
| **Inrō** | `inro.social` | Instagram DM automation with lead capture. |

This list was set on 2026-09-26 and has **not been benchmarked yet** — confirm each URL with `WebSearch` before relying on it, and add or drop competitors the user names. After the first real benchmark, fill in the "Watch for" detail below from what you actually read.

## Method

1. **Search, don't guess.** `WebSearch` for `<competitor> help getting started instagram comment automation`, then `WebFetch` the actual quick-start or help page. Their structure is the data; secondhand summaries are not.
2. **Record the step order, not the prose.** What do they make you do first? Where do account-type prerequisites appear? Where does pricing appear?
3. **Find the drop-off guards.** "Before you begin" warnings, account-type checks, "why isn't my automation firing" troubleshooting. These exist because their support team got tired of the same ticket — free research.
4. **Find what they avoid saying.** If nobody explains *why* Instagram restricts automated DMs, or what happens after the messaging window closes, that's a gap we can own.
5. **Cross-check every claimed advantage against the product** before it goes in a page — see `docs-fact-check`. An advantage we don't actually ship is worse than no advantage.

## Patterns already adopted

From the WaMatrix.io benchmark, applied here by default (`comentify-docs` §4):

- **Prerequisites first** — the "Before you start" section.
- **Checklist framing** — numbered `<Steps>`, a `<Check>` recap, then "Keep going" cards.
- **A migration answer** — "I already use another tool, can I move across?" in the FAQ, once we know what the honest answer is.
- **Rejected:** feature dumps and jargon. Outcomes, not module names.

## Our honest differentiators

**None verified yet.** Candidates to check against the product and the competitors — do not publish any of these until both checks pass:

- Facebook Pages *and* Instagram in one place (several competitors are Instagram-only).
- A shared team inbox alongside the automations.
- AI reply suggestions, and which plans include them.

**Rules when using differentiators:** never name a competitor on a docs page — describe the benefit, not the comparison. Never state a competitor's pricing or limits; they change and being wrong is worse than being silent. Re-verify a differentiator before reusing it.
